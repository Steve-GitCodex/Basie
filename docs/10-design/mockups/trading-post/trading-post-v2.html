1One featured slot. Same-size, aligned items all read as equally important; give the best offer its own slot, bigger.
2Free daily crate + red dot. A red dot pulls players in to claim something free. That builds a daily return habit with no spending pressure.
3"For you" row. Research says tailored offers do better than a full catalogue. Single-player, we can do it from game state: running queues, lowest resource, a hero near level-up. Each card says why it's suggested.
4No more than 6–8 per row. More than about 8 offers reads as endless. 17 speed-ups become 5 durations plus a type switch.
Own + Use on the card. Shows what you already have and spends it right there, with no detour through Inventory.
Every price is a labelled button with its currency, greyed out when you can't afford it. No hidden close buttons, no "No, I like losing" wording.

5Exchange widget. Pick a resource to give, one to get, and slide the amount (as Heroes of Might & Magic and Travian do). It replaces 6 fixed cards and covers every pair. Rates come from one table with a spread, so a wood↔stone round trip always loses, which closes the known tradeBonus exploit by design. The existing inflation shows as a meter, not a warning line.
6Wandering Trader (Rise of Kingdoms' Mysterious Merchant). Rotating stock bought with resources, so surplus wood and food become useful items. It comes back faster when you build, train or fight, which rewards playing. This is new mechanics: stock generation, refresh timer, save state.
Why merge? Shop = coins/diamonds → items; Market = resources → resources/items. Under one roof with tabs, players have one place to go for anything they want to buy.

3 · Premium (diamonds) — honest by default

There is no payment backend, so "$4.99" buttons promise something the game can't deliver. Options: hide the cash packs until Phase 7, or keep them clearly labelled as a demo. Diamond items (queue expansions, instant finish) stay in Supply with a confirm step.
