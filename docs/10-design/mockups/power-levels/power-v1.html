POWER & LEVELS — v1 prototypes

These screens follow the decisions already made. Power is combat-weighted: buildings and other research count but are capped, and wounded troops don't count until healed. Power shows in the top bar and profile, as recommended power on enemies, and in battle reports. Player level is an activity and rewards meter, and HQ stays the gate. Click a card to select it. Click a category row to expand it.

All numbers are placeholders until the formula draft (docs/research/power-levels-formula.md) lands.

1 · Profile: power breakdown (A) inside the current profile window

This section is built on the profile window as it exists today. It has a crown icon, your name and "Level N Commander" at the top, four button tabs (Profile · Achievements · Account · Settings), and a Profile tab holding an XP bar and 12 Statistics rows. The chosen breakdown (A) needs a home in it.

PLACEMENT 1 — Inside the Profile tab · CHOSEN

The Profile tab becomes three blocks. Level: the existing XP bar, plus the level cap and XP bank line. Power: breakdown A. Statistics: the existing 12 rows, unchanged and collapsible. The tab row stays at four.

Pros: no new tab, and level, power and stats read as one "commander sheet". The header can show power next to the level.
Cons: a long tab on a phone. Statistics is collapsed by default to keep it short.
Build note: SettingsUI.js is 513 lines, so the profile modal moves into js/ui/profile/ first.

PLACEMENT 2 — A fifth "Power" tab

Breakdown A gets its own tab next to Profile. The Profile tab keeps the level and Statistics as they are today.

Pros: breakdown A gets the whole tab, and the Profile tab barely changes.
Cons: five button tabs are cramped at 360px. Power is one tap further away.

2 · Recommended power on enemies

OPTION A — Coloured power chip

Each campaign stage and world monster shows a chip with its recommended power, coloured by how your power compares. Green means you're comfortably stronger, amber means it's even, and red means you'll probably lose. The bands are placeholders.

Pros: compact and fits the existing stage cards and POI panel.
Cons: one number hides the counter matchups. A hint line can name the enemy's main troop type.

OPTION B — You-vs-enemy gauge · CHOSEN

A gauge with red, amber and green zones and a marker for your squad's power, with a one-word verdict: Risky, Even or Favoured, plus the existing simulated win %. Tapping a marker pops the card attached to that target, with an arrow pointing at it. The card flips above or below and stays inside the screen. When you drag the map it follows the target, and it hides if the target goes off screen. List cards and markers keep the chip from A. World map only: the campaign keeps its own stage panel (battle-tab.md), with the same gauge placed inside it.

Pros: the clearest picture before you commit troops, and it uses the squad you're sending, not your whole power.
Cons: takes more space, so it only fits panels, not list cards.

3 · Level up

OPTION A — Celebration card · CHOSEN

A centred card with the new level, its rewards, and the next milestone. Collect sends the rewards straight to the bag. Milestone levels get a highlighted reward. The level-up mail goes away. Level-ups are queued: during a critical moment the plate shows a waiting pip instead. When the moment ends, the queue releases as a deck of cards, one per level. Collect takes that level's rewards and shows the next card; Collect all clears the deck. Try it in the desktop frame.

Critical moments (the queue holds): the battle scene or playback, a tutorial step, a confirm dialog, or any open modal.
Deck: the lowest level is on top, with "1 of N" shown. Closing the deck without collecting keeps the rewards waiting behind the plate pip, so nothing is lost.
Pros: a satisfying moment for every level, and it never cuts into a fight.
Cons: a long deck after a big XP jump. Collect all keeps it to one tap.

OPTION B — Top banner with auto-collect

A banner slides in under the header with the level and its rewards, then auto-collects. Milestone levels still open the card from A. The top-bar plate shows the XP bar and the power change. Level-ups are queued: during a critical moment the plate shows a waiting pip instead. When the moment ends, the queue releases one stacked banner per level.

Critical moments (queue holds): battle scene or playback, tutorial step, a confirm dialog, and an open modal for milestone cards only.
Stacking: at most 3 banners show at once, with "+N more" for the rest. Each banner auto-collects its own level's rewards. A milestone card waits until the stack has cleared.
Pros: never interrupts, and each level still gets its moment.
Cons: ordinary level-ups are easy to miss, so the rewards also go in the Inventory "NEW" dot.
